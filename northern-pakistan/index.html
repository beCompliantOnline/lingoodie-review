The trip at a glance

Sara lands Sep 10 (one night in Islamabad) and flies up to Gilgit with Larisa on Sep 11 — two easy days before Antonio arrives. He lands early on Sep 13 and flies straight up, and from there all three travel together: seven days around Gilgit & Hunza (forts, Gojal, Khunjerab and a Fairy Meadows side-trip), one long drive east to Skardu, a buffer day, then the direct Skardu → Lahore flight and home on Sep 23.

Two short flights bookend the mountains and a private 4×4 covers the scenic overland stretches. Fairy Meadows is kept as a 2-night side-trip inside the Hunza week (the one long drive + hike in that stretch). The Skardu buffer day before the flight out stays put. Anything marked amber is optional — drop it for a gentler pace, or keep it if you're up for more.

Why this order

Airblue flies Skardu → Lahore nonstop (PA-705, ~PKR 24,900), so the route runs west→east — fly up to Gilgit, sweep through Hunza, Fairy Meadows and Baltistan, then take the reliable direct hop from Skardu to Lahore. No overland grind, no Islamabad connection at the end.

Smart risk placement

Mountain flights can slip for weather, so the flaky one sits at the start (Gilgit-in — if it cancels you just drive up). The end uses the reliable direct Skardu→Lahore, with a Skardu buffer day before it — nothing threatens your flight home.

⚠️ Safety, health & season

Weather this year — cool & wet up high

The current forecast has a cold, unsettled spell through the middle of the trip: the high valleys are running below the September average, and Fairy Meadows / the base-camp day (Sep 16–17) may see snow showers with near-freezing nights. Pack properly warm and waterproof for the hiking days, and don't push to base camp if it's snowing up high — the LIVE line on each day shows the latest.

Mid-September timing & the flights

Hunza's golden autumn is beginning. The flaky mountain flight (Gilgit-in) sits at the start, where a cancellation just means driving up. The exit is the reliable direct Skardu→Lahore, with the Skardu buffer day in front of it — so nothing threatens your Sep 23 flights home.

Health & mosquitoes

Almost all of this trip is at altitude, where mosquito-borne illness is a non-issue. For the low-elevation hours (Islamabad on Sep 10, Lahore on Sep 22–23), use DEET repellent and long sleeves at dawn/dusk as your travel clinic advised. Everywhere else you're high enough that it's simply not a concern.

Pakistan carries a US "Level 3 – Reconsider Travel" advisory; Gilgit-Baltistan is among the more tourist-friendly regions. Use a reputable driver-guide, travel by day, and check your own government's advisory and insurance before booking.

Practical notes

🚙 Fly the dull legs, drive the beautiful ones

A hired 4×4 with driver covers the scenic overland stretches (Hunza gorges, the Indus into Baltistan, up to Fairy Meadows). The two short flights skip the long, unremarkable hauls in and out. No car of your own? Mountain Dale and your Hunza guesthouse can arrange a driver for the day trips that need one — and a Gilgit travel agency can bundle the whole circuit.

✈️ Keep the Gilgit flight flexible; hold the Skardu buffer

If PK-603 is scrubbed, start driving up instead (long, but it's early). Don't give up the Sep 21 Skardu buffer — it's your insurance for the direct Lahore flight.

🎒 Packing

Layers for big day–night swings, a genuinely warm + waterproof jacket (this year's forecast is cool and wet up high), proper hiking boots for the base-camp day, sun protection, a power bank, and cash — ATMs thin out beyond Gilgit and Skardu.
